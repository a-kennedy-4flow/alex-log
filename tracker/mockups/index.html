Seven mockups of the same month. May 2026 in DE_BERLIN. One dataset drives all of them so nothing but the design differs. Three further pages take the Jira screen.

The Jira screen

A button copies the result into the month. H1 is the design. It wins because it is the only one of the three that holds all three tables at the full width. Jira cannot say how long anything took

The site holds no worklog for this user in August 2026 and timespent is empty on all seven tickets. So the hours column is typed by the user on every row today. Five of the seven resolution dates also fall inside two minutes on 13 August because they were closed in one bulk action. A resolution date records when a ticket was closed and not when the work happened. Jira supplies the text and the project. The judgement stays with the user.

Rounding up runs per Workday ID

Fifty hours is 6.25 days. Rounding each Workday ID up to the nearest half day on its own books 6.5. That gap grows with the number of Workday IDs so both figures are on the page. The target for August at an 80 per cent contract is 16.8 days so the fill covers part of the month and says so.

What all seven obey

  • Arial only. Nothing below 13 px.
  • Theme colours only. No shade was invented.
  • One corner radius per mockup applied to every box. That is what Correct corners does to a deck.
  • Bright Blue on internal table lines only. No outer border on any table.
  • Vibrant Orange reserved for the primary action and the one figure that is off target.
  • Content sits inside a design area with a held margin.
  • Every figure is tabular so columns of numbers line up.

The palette cannot colour code

D and E give each cost centre a fill. The theme holds five usable ones so five cost centres is the limit before the fills stop separating. Two of those five are tints mixed from Bright Blue and Vibrant Orange. The deck forbids inventing a shade so a brand owner has to approve them. A and B and C and F avoid the problem by never colouring a cost centre. Decision 2 above puts the three ways out side by side.

One brand rule fights accessibility

White on Vibrant Orange reaches only 3.3 to 1. Body text needs 4.5 to 1. Smart Blue on that same orange reaches 4.7 to 1. So every primary button here sets dark text on orange. Bright Blue on white reaches 3.9 to 1 which is why it draws lines and never carries text.

Entering a share on quick fill

Five ways to give a cost centre its share of the month and the box the page has today to measure them against. Option B won and is built. Because a) the arithmetic of making four figures reach a hundred is the complaint the 100 / n button was added for. b) B is the only one of the five that removes that arithmetic rather than checking it afterwards, because the total cannot leave a hundred. c) the shares are a split of one month and a control that can express nothing else says so. It costs predictability, and Hold is what pays it. Every page reads the same four cost centres and sits in the same sheet so the share column is the only thing that differs. The four fills option C needs were computed with contrast.py and are written on that page. The four that lost are kept because a decision is only readable beside what it beat.
