# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English — writing rules

All 53 writing rules of ASD-STE100 Issue 9 (2025-01-15), part 1 - Writing rules, one file per rule.
Extracted by `ste/tools/extract_rules.py`.

## Section 1 – Words

- [Rule 1.1](rule-1.01-use-words-that-are-approved-in-the.md) — Use words that are: - Approved in the dictionary - Technical nouns - Technical verbs.
- [Rule 1.2](rule-1.02-use-approved-words-from-the-dictionary-only.md) — Use approved words from the dictionary only as the specified part of speech.
- [Rule 1.3](rule-1.03-use-approved-words-only-with-their-approved.md) — Use approved words only with their approved meanings.
- [Rule 1.4](rule-1.04-use-only-the-approved-forms-of-verbs.md) — Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives.
- [Rule 1.5](rule-1.05-you-can-use-words-that-you-can.md) — You can use words that you can include in a technical noun category.
- [Rule 1.6](rule-1.06-use-a-word-that-is-not-approved.md) — Use a word that is not approved in the dictionary, only when it is a technical noun or part of a technical noun.
- [Rule 1.7](rule-1.07-do-not-use-words-that-are-technical.md) — Do not use words that are technical nouns as verbs.
- [Rule 1.8](rule-1.08-use-technical-nouns-that-are-approved-in.md) — Use technical nouns that are approved in your company, industry, or subject field.
- [Rule 1.9](rule-1.09-when-you-must-select-a-technical-noun.md) — When you must select a technical noun, use one which is short and easy to understand.
- [Rule 1.10](rule-1.10-do-not-use-regional-slang-or-jargon.md) — Do not use regional, slang, or jargon words as technical nouns.
- [Rule 1.11](rule-1.11-do-not-use-different-technical-nouns-for.md) — Do not use different technical nouns for the same item.
- [Rule 1.12](rule-1.12-you-can-use-verbs-that-you-can.md) — You can use verbs that you can include in a technical verb category.
- [Rule 1.13](rule-1.13-do-not-use-technical-verbs-as-nouns.md) — Do not use technical verbs as nouns.
- [Rule 1.14](rule-1.14-use-american-english-spelling-unless-other-official.md) — Use American English spelling unless other official directives tell you differently.

## Section 2 – Multi-word nouns

- [Rule 2.1](rule-2.01-write-multi-word-nouns-of-no-more.md) — Write multi-word nouns of no more than three words.
- [Rule 2.2](rule-2.02-when-a-technical-noun-has-more-than.md) — When a technical noun has more than three words, write it in full. Then, you can use one of these methods to make the technical noun clear: - Give a shorter form of the technical noun. - Use hyphens (-) between words that you use as one unit.

## Section 3 – Verbs

- [Rule 3.1](rule-3.01-use-only-the-verb-forms-that-are.md) — Use only the verb forms that are given in the dictionary.
- [Rule 3.2](rule-3.02-use-only-these-verb-forms-and-tenses.md) — Use only these verb forms and tenses of verbs: - The infinitive form - The imperative form (command form) - The simple present tense - The simple past tense - The simple future tense - The past participle form (as an adjective).
- [Rule 3.3](rule-3.03-use-the-past-participle-form-as-an.md) — Use the past participle form as an adjective.
- [Rule 3.4](rule-3.04-do-not-use-auxiliary-verbs-to-make.md) — Do not use auxiliary verbs to make complex verb constructions.
- [Rule 3.5](rule-3.05-use-the-ing-form-of-a-verb.md) — Use the “-ing” form of a verb only as a technical noun or as a modifier in a technical noun.
- [Rule 3.6](rule-3.06-use-the-active-voice-in-descriptive-writing.md) — Use the active voice. In descriptive writing, you can use the passive voice only if the agent is unknown.
- [Rule 3.7](rule-3.07-use-an-approved-verb-to-describe-an.md) — Use an approved verb to describe an action, not a noun or other parts of speech.

## Section 4 – Sentences

- [Introduction](section-4-sentences-introduction.md)
- [Rule 4.1](rule-4.01-write-short-and-clear-sentences.md) — Write short and clear sentences.
- [Rule 4.2](rule-4.02-do-not-omit-words-or-use-contractions.md) — Do not omit words or use contractions to make your sentences shorter.
- [Rule 4.3](rule-4.03-use-a-vertical-list-for-complex-text.md) — Use a vertical list for complex text.
- [Rule 4.4](rule-4.04-use-connecting-words-and-connecting-phrases-to.md) — Use connecting words and connecting phrases to connect sentences that contain related topics.
- [Rule 4.5](rule-4.05-when-applicable-use-an-article-the-a.md) — When applicable, use an article (the, a, an) or a demonstrative adjective (this, these) before a noun or a multi-word noun.

## Section 5 – Procedural writing

- [Rule 5.1](rule-5.01-write-short-sentences-use-a-maximum-of.md) — Write short sentences. Use a maximum of 20 words in each sentence.
- [Rule 5.2](rule-5.02-write-only-one-instruction-in-each-sentence.md) — Write only one instruction in each sentence unless two or more actions occur at the same time.
- [Rule 5.3](rule-5.03-write-instructions-in-the-imperative-command-form.md) — Write instructions in the imperative (command) form.
- [Rule 5.4](rule-5.04-when-there-is-a-condition-that-the.md) — When there is a condition that the reader must know about first, start the instruction with a descriptive statement. Then, divide that descriptive statement from the command with a comma.
- [Rule 5.5](rule-5.05-write-notes-only-to-give-information-not.md) — Write notes only to give information, not instructions.

- [Rule 6.1](rule-6.01-give-information-gradually.md) — Give information gradually.

## Section 7 – Safety instructions

- [Introduction](section-7-safety-instructions-introduction.md)
- [Rule 7.1](rule-7.01-use-an-applicable-word-for-example-warning.md) — Use an applicable word (for example, “warning” or “caution”) to identify the level of risk.
- [Rule 7.2](rule-7.02-start-a-safety-instruction-with-a-clear.md) — Start a safety instruction with a clear and accurate command or condition.

## Section 8 – Punctuation and word count

- [Introduction](section-8-punctuation-and-word-count-introduction.md)
- [Rule 8.1](rule-8.01-you-can-use-all-standard-english-punctuation.md) — You can use all standard English punctuation marks but not the semicolon (;).
- [Rule 8.2](rule-8.02-use-hyphens-to-connect-words-that-are.md) — Use hyphens (-) to connect words that are directly related.
- [Rule 8.3](rule-8.03-you-can-use-parentheses-to-make-references.md) — You can use parentheses: - To make references to illustrations or text - To include letters or numbers that identify items on an illustration or in a text - To identify the work steps in a procedure - To include abbreviations - To give the singular and plural forms of a noun at the same time - To explain words or a part of a sentence - To include an alternative.
- [Rule 8.4](rule-8.04-in-a-vertical-list-a-colon-has.md) — In a vertical list, a colon (:) has the same effect on word count as a period and shows the end of a sentence.
- [Rule 8.5](rule-8.05-when-you-put-text-in-parentheses-it.md) — When you put text in parentheses, it counts as one word in that sentence.
- [Rule 8.6](rule-8.06-count-each-of-these-elements-as-one.md) — Count each of these elements as one word: - Numbers - Numbers together with units of measurement - Abbreviations - Alphanumeric identifiers - Quoted text - Titles, headings, and text on placards and labels - Proper nouns of individuals, groups, organizations, and geopolitical entities.
- [Rule 8.7](rule-8.07-hyphenated-words-count-as-one-word.md) — Hyphenated words count as one word.
