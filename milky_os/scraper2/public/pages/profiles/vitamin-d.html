What it is

Vitamin D is a group of structurally related, fat-soluble compounds responsible for increasing intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate, along with numerous other biological functions. In humans, the most important compounds within this group are vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) and vitamin D2 (ergocalciferol). The name Vitamin D is a misnomer; technically vitamin D is not a vitamin, but rather a fat-soluble secosteroid hormone.

The primary source of vitamin D is cutaneous synthesis upon exposure to ultraviolet B (UVB) light; dietary intake is a secondary source. Human milk alone does not provide sufficient vitamin D to meet infant needs. The IOM set the AI for vitamin D to 400 IU/d (10 microgram/d) for infants under 12 mo based on the maintenance of serum 25(OH)D concentration over 30 nmol/L; clearly, human milk provides only a small fraction of this requirement.
