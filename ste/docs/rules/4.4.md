# Rule 4.4 — Connecting words and phrases

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-4-7)
> Use connecting words and connecting phrases to connect sentences that contain
> related topics.

- **Applies to:** consecutive sentences of a descriptive text, and explanations
  after a work step or inside a safety instruction.
- **Needs:** text, judgment (which topics are related).
- **Decidable:** reporting only. A checker can say that a paragraph has no
  connecting word at all; it cannot say that a missing one was needed.
- **Code:** not implemented.

## Discrete form

```
check(text, structure S) -> [report]

for each paragraph p of S with 3 or more sentences:
    if no sentence of p after the first starts with a connecting word or phrase,
       and no sentence uses a demonstrative adjective to refer back:
        report(p, :no_connection_between_sentences, severity: :recommendation)

pass = always true; this rule reports, it does not fail a text
```

## The connectors that the standard names

| Kind | Words |
| --- | --- |
| connecting words (approved in the dictionary) | and, but, then, thus |
| connecting phrases | as a result, at the same time |
| demonstrative adjectives used as connectors | this, these ("This method will help you...", "THIS PRECAUTION WILL PREVENT INJURY.") |

## Examples in STE

- `The localizer course aligns with the centerline of the runway. And the
  glideslope path is at a constant angle to the threshold of the runway.`
- `These safety precautions are the minimum necessary for work in the pit lane.
  But the local regulations can give other necessary safety precautions.`
- `If the pressure increases, it changes the electrical currents in the
  transmitter. Thus, the power unit supplies currents to the indicator...`
- `When the hydraulic pressure is released, the sequence valve moves to the open
  position. As a result, the actuators are connected to the hydraulic return.`
- `Tag the disassembled parts. This method will help you during the subsequent
  procedures.`

## Notes

- STE permits "And" and "But" at the start of a sentence, which standard style
  guides often do not. A checker that carries a general English style rule must
  switch that rule off.
- The rule is the reason rule 8.1 can forbid the semicolon without making texts
  harder to read: two sentences plus a connector replace it.
