# Rule 4.2 — No omitted words, no contractions

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-4-3)
> Do not omit words or use contractions to make your sentences shorter.

- **Applies to:** every sentence.
- **Needs:** text (contractions), parser (omissions).
- **Decidable:** the contractions yes, exactly. The omissions need a parser that
  can say a sentence has no subject or no verb.
- **Code:** not implemented.

## Discrete form

```
check(text) -> [finding]

for each token t of text:
    if t is a contraction:  finding(t, use: expansion(t))

for each sentence s of text:
    if s has no subject:                     finding(s, :subject_omitted)
    if s has no finite verb and is not a
       title, heading, or vertical list item: finding(s, :verb_omitted)
    if a noun of s has no article and needs one:  → rule 4.5

pass = findings == []
```

## Contractions

`don't, doesn't, isn't, aren't, wasn't, weren't, can't, won't, shouldn't,
couldn't, wouldn't, it's, that's, there's, you're, we're, they're, I'm, let's,
hasn't, haven't, hadn't, didn't, mustn't`

"Cannot" is not a contraction and the dictionary approves it.

Test: any apostrophe followed by `t`, `s`, `re`, `ve`, `ll`, `m`, `d` where the
token is not a possessive of a proper noun.

## Examples

| Verdict | Text | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pass | `If your hands are wet, do not touch the USB power adapter.` | written in full |
| pass | `Cracks can have a maximum length of five inches.` | subject and verb present |
| fail | `If your hands are wet, don't touch the USB power adapter.` | contraction |
| fail | `Can be a maximum of five inches long.` | no subject → `Cracks can have a maximum length of five inches.` |
| fail | `Rotary switch to INPUT.` | no verb → `Set the rotary switch to INPUT.` |
| fail | `If installed, remove the shims.` | no subject in the condition → `If shims are installed, remove them.` |
| fail | `Remove the bolt and stop.` | omitted article makes "stop" ambiguous → `Remove the bolt and the stop.` |
| fail | `WARNING: ...IS NOT PRESSURIZED. IF NOT, THIS CAN CAUSE INJURY TO PERSONS.` | omitted subject → `A PRESSURIZED SYSTEM CAN CAUSE INJURY TO PERSONS.` |

## Notes

- The last example is a safety instruction, and the standard shows it under this
  rule because an omitted subject there changes what the reader thinks the risk is.
  A checker should raise the severity of an omission finding inside a warning or a
  caution.
- The article half of the rule belongs to rule 4.5; keep the finding there so a
  writer sees one message per problem.
