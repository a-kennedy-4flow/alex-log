# Rule 3.3 — The past participle as an adjective

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-3-2)
> Use the past participle form as an adjective.

- **Applies to:** every past participle of the text.
- **Needs:** dictionary, parser (position of the participle).
- **Decidable:** with a tagger. The two permitted positions are simple to test
  once the participle and its neighbours are known.
- **Code:** not implemented.

## Discrete form

```
check(text, dictionary D) -> [finding]

for each past participle p of text:
    if D does not list p as an approved form or as an approved adjective:
        finding(p, reason: :form_not_in_dictionary)      # rules 3.1 and 1.4
    else if p is immediately before a noun:               continue
    else if p is after a form of "to be", "to become", or "to stay": continue
    else: finding(p, reason: :not_used_as_an_adjective)

pass = findings == []
```

## Examples

| Verdict | Text | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pass | `Examine all parts of the disassembled unit for damage.` | adjective before a noun |
| pass | `When the unit is fully disassembled, clean all the parts.` | adjective after "to be", showing a condition |
| pass | `Do not put more than the permitted weight on the trolley.` | "permitted" is an approved adjective whose verb is not approved |
| pass | `Make sure that the mating surfaces are not damaged.` | "damaged" is an approved adjective |
| fail | `The operator has adjusted the linkage.` | the participle is a verb form here (rules 3.2 and 3.4) |
| fail | `The volume control can be adjusted.` | passive voice (rules 3.4 and 3.6) |

## Notes

- "This is not passive voice" is the sentence the standard uses to separate this
  rule from rule 3.6: `the unit is disassembled` describes a condition, while
  `the unit is disassembled by the operator` describes an action. The presence of
  an agent, or of an adverb of manner, is the signal that a checker can use.
- Some approved adjectives are the past participle of verbs that are **not**
  approved ("permitted", "damaged"). They are approved as `(adj)` in the
  dictionary, so the test is a dictionary lookup by part of speech, not
  morphology.
