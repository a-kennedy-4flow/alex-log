# Rule 7.3 — Explain the risk

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-7-4)
> Give an explanation to show the risk or possible result.

- **Applies to:** every safety instruction.
- **Needs:** structure; a pattern list for the consequence sentence.
- **Decidable:** heuristic. The consequence sentence has a small number of shapes
  in STE, because the vocabulary is controlled.
- **Code:** not implemented.

## Discrete form

```
check(text, structure S) -> [finding]

for each safety instruction b of S:
    if a sentence of b after the command states a consequence:  continue
    else: finding(b, :no_explanation_of_the_risk)

consequence patterns:
    "<subject> can cause <result>"        SOLVENTS ARE POISONOUS AND CAN CAUSE INJURY OR DEATH.
    "<result> can occur"                  PERMANENT DAMAGE TO THE PARTS CAN OCCUR.
    "this precaution will prevent <x>"    THIS PRECAUTION WILL PREVENT DAMAGE TO THE UNIT.
    "there is a risk of <x>"              THERE IS A RISK OF SUFFOCATION.
```

## Examples

All of these pass, with the explanation underlined in the standard:

- `WARNING: DO NOT SWALLOW THE SOLVENT. ... SOLVENTS ARE POISONOUS AND CAN CAUSE
  INJURY OR DEATH.`
- `CAUTION: DO NOT USE BLEACH OR CLEANSERS THAT CONTAIN CHLORINE TO CLEAN THE UNIT.
  THESE CLEANING AGENTS CAN CAUSE CORROSION.`
- `WARNING: WHILE YOU USE THE SPRAY PAINT, POINT THE SPRAY AWAY FROM YOUR FACE. THE
  SPRAY PAINT CAN CAUSE INJURY TO YOUR EYES.`
- `CAUTION: WHEN YOU ASSEMBLE THE UNIT, DO NOT LET THE PARTS FALL. IF THEY FALL,
  PERMANENT DAMAGE TO THE PARTS CAN OCCUR.`

## Notes

- The rule says "if it is possible", so a safety instruction with no explanation is
  a question and not always an error. Report it with a lower severity than a
  missing signal word (rule 7.1).
- The three rules of section 7 make a template that a checker can test as a whole:
  signal word, then command or condition, then consequence. Reporting the template
  slot that is missing is more useful than three separate findings.
