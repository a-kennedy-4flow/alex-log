# Rule 6.1 — Give information gradually

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-6-1)
> Give information gradually.

- **Applies to:** a descriptive text as a whole.
- **Needs:** judgment.
- **Decidable:** no. What a checker can measure is in rules 6.3, 6.5, 6.6, and
  6.2.
- **Code:** not implemented, and should not be.

## Discrete form

```
check(text, structure S) -> [report]

for each sentence s of a descriptive text:
    if s has more than one subject:  report(s, :more_than_one_topic)   # rule 6.5
    if s is in the imperative:       finding(s, :imperative_in_descriptive_writing)

for each paragraph p:
    report the chain: does each sentence after the first take a key word from an
    earlier sentence?                                                 # rule 6.2

pass = only the imperative finding can fail a text here
```

The one part of this rule that is a hard verdict is that descriptive writing does
not use the imperative form. The rest is a review question.

## The example, both ways

Not STE (the first sentence starts in the middle of the subject, and the topic
changes three times):

> During the approach to the runway, deviation pointers in the course indicators
> give commands to fly up or down and left or right. This information comes from
> the VHF transceivers, which are part of the Instrument Landing System. ...

STE (each sentence has one subject, and each takes a key word from the sentence
before it):

> The Instrument Landing System (the system) on the aircraft shows data that helps
> the pilot during the approach to the runway. This system shows the pilot the
> deviations from the localizer course and the glideslope path. The localizer
> course aligns with the centerline of the runway. And the glideslope path is at a
> constant angle to the threshold of the runway. During the approach to the runway,
> deviation pointers in the course indicators show the pilot in which direction the
> aircraft must go:
> - Left or right (for the localizer)
> - Up or down (for the glideslope).
>
> This data about deviations from the localizer course and glideslope path comes
> from two VHF transceivers. These transceivers transmit this data to Air Traffic
> Control.

Five sentences in the first paragraph, two in the second, which is rules 6.4 and
6.6 at work.

## Notes

- Descriptive writing is: a description of an item, product, system, or component
  and how it operates; a text that gives general information (reports, brochures,
  papers); and a note in a procedure. The last one is why rule 5.5 sends notes to
  the descriptive rules.
