# Rule 8.6 — Elements that count as one word

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-8-5)
> Count each of these elements as one word:
> - Numbers
> - Numbers together with units of measurement
> - Abbreviations
> - Alphanumeric identifiers
> - Quoted text
> - Titles, headings, and text on placards and labels
> - Proper nouns of individuals, groups, organizations, and geopolitical entities.

- **Applies to:** the word count, and (as a side effect) to which tokens the
  vocabulary rules apply to at all.
- **Needs:** text; a list of unit symbols; structure for titles and headings.
- **Decidable:** yes for numbers, units, identifiers, and quoted text. Titles and
  proper nouns of organizations need to be marked up or listed.
- **Code:** `Ste.Text` implements the tokenizing half: it classifies these elements
  as `:number`, `:identifier`, `:caps`, or `:quoted` so that rule 1.1 does not treat
  them as vocabulary.

## Discrete form

```
ste_word_count(sentence):
    each of these counts as 1 word:
      a number, in digits or in words                    13, 16, twenty-one
      a number together with its unit                    10 °C, 20 kg, 10 mA, 6 mm
      an abbreviation, acronym, or initialism            a.m., NASA, VPN
      an alphanumeric identifier                         No. 1, 36L7, L42
      quoted text, in quotation marks or in uppercase     "Service Overview",
                                                          SHORT-CIRCUIT TEST
      a formula                                          C = (A - B) - 0.063 mm
      a title, heading, placard, or label                Structural Repair Manual,
                                                          Testing and Fault Isolation
      a proper noun of an individual, group,             George Washington,
        organization, or geopolitical entity              United States of America

    a number that identifies a paragraph or a work step is not counted at all
```

## Notes

- The uppercase case is the one that carries the most weight for a checker: STE
  says that uppercase letters or a different font can show quoted text, so a run
  of uppercase words is one word **and** is not vocabulary. That is why rule 1.1
  skips it instead of reporting `SHORT-CIRCUIT` as an unknown word.
- Titles, headings, placards, and proper nouns of organizations cannot be found in
  a plain string. They need markup, a list, or a convention, and until then a
  counter will over-count them. Say so in the output rather than guessing.
