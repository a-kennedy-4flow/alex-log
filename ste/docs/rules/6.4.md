# Rule 6.4 — Paragraphs

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-6-5)
> Use paragraphs to show related information.

- **Applies to:** a descriptive text as a whole.
- **Needs:** structure, judgment.
- **Decidable:** no on its own. Its measurable companions are rule 6.5 (one topic)
  and rule 6.6 (at most six sentences).
- **Code:** not implemented.

## Discrete form

```
check(text, structure S) -> [report]

if the descriptive text has more than 6 sentences and no paragraph break:
    report(:no_paragraphs)
for each paragraph:
    6.5(paragraph) ++ 6.6(paragraph)
```

## What the standard asks for

- Paragraphs divide a text into logical units and keep the reader's attention.
- Do not put different topics in the same paragraph.
- If a paragraph has more than six sentences, divide it into two smaller
  paragraphs.

The worked example (the fuel manifold description) shows the shape it is asking
for: paragraphs of 1, 2, 5, 4, 4, and 2 sentences, each about one part of the unit,
with vertical lists for the parts of each part.

## Notes

- A checker that only counts sentences per paragraph implements rule 6.6 and calls
  it 6.4. Keep them separate: 6.6 is a count, 6.4 is about whether the division
  follows the content, which is why the standard states them as two rules and then
  explains them together.
