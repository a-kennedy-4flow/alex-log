# Rule 2.1 — Multi-word nouns

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-2-1)
> Write multi-word nouns of no more than three words.

- **Applies to:** every group of nouns and adjectives that has the function of one
  part of speech in the sentence.
- **Needs:** parser (noun-phrase chunking), glossary (an official technical noun
  is rule 2.2's business, not this rule's).
- **Decidable:** with a chunker. The counting is exact once the group is found.
- **Code:** not implemented. `Ste.Text` already counts hyphenated words as one
  word (rule 8.7).

## Discrete form

```
check(text, glossary G) -> [finding]

for each multi-word noun m of text (a run of nouns and adjectives that acts as
                                    the subject or object):
    if G lists m as an official technical noun:  continue    # rule 2.2 applies
    if word_count(m) > 3:  finding(m, count: word_count(m))

pass = findings == []

word_count: hyphenated groups count as one word (rule 8.7)
```

## Examples

| Verdict | Text | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pass | `Actuator operating rod` | 3 |
| pass | `Runway light connection` | 3 |
| pass | `Make sure that the cutoff-switch power connection is safe.` | 3, because of the hyphen |
| pass | `Move the main-gear-door retraction-winch handle.` | 3, because of the hyphens |
| fail | `Horizontal cylinder pivot bearing` | 4 |
| fail | `Runway light connection resistance calibration` | 5 → `Calibration of the resistance of the runway light connection.` |
| fail | `Stainless steel corrosion protection strips` | 5 |
| fail | `Install the forward turbine overheat thermocouple terminal tags.` | 6 → `Install the terminal tags on the forward overheat thermocouple of the turbine.` |
| fail | `Move the main-gear-door-retraction-winch handle.` | 2 by the count, but the standard rejects it: hyphens must not make groups of more than three words |

## Notes

- The last example is the one place where the word count and the rule disagree.
  Read the rule as: at most three words **and** at most three hyphen-connected
  units, and no unit that connects words which are not related (rule 8.2).
- The repair the standard shows is to use prepositions ("of", "on", "in", "for")
  to break the group up, which makes the sentence longer in words but shorter in
  modifiers. That trade is why this rule and rule 5.1 have to be checked together.
- The main noun is usually the last word of the group. A finding is more useful
  when it names that head noun, because that is what the writer must keep.
