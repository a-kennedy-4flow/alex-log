# Rule 8.5 — Text in parentheses

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-8-5)
> When you put text in parentheses, it counts as one word in that sentence.

- **Applies to:** the word count.
- **Needs:** text.
- **Decidable:** yes. This is a counter rule.
- **Code:** not implemented; `Ste.Text` tokenizes the parenthetical content but
  does not yet count it as one word.

## Discrete form

```
ste_word_count(sentence):
    for each parenthesis pair p in the sentence:
        p counts as 1 word of this sentence
        the words inside p also make a sentence of their own, counted separately
        with the same limit
```

## Examples with their counts

| Text | Count | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Make sure that the EMER pushbutton switch is released (the EMER legend is off).` | 10 | the parenthesis is 1 word; the sentence inside has 5 words and is counted on its own |
| `Remove the safety pin (10).` | 5 | `(10)` is an identifier, 1 word |
| `Installation of a Business Class (B/C) Seat` | 7 | `(B/C)` is an abbreviation, 1 word |
| `Hardware and Software Configuration Check of the In-Flight Entertainment (IFE) System` | 11 | `(IFE)` is 1 word, `In-Flight` is 1 word (rule 8.7) |

## Notes

- The rule cuts both ways: a long explanation in parentheses does not make its
  sentence fail rule 5.1, but it is a sentence of its own and must obey the same
  limit. A checker that only counts the outer sentence lets a 40-word parenthesis
  through.
- Rule 8.3 says what parentheses may be used for; this rule says only how to count
  them.
