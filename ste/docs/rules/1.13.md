# Rule 1.13 — Technical verbs as nouns

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-1-14)
> Do not use technical verbs as nouns.

- **Applies to:** every technical verb of the glossary that the text uses.
- **Needs:** glossary, parser (part of speech).
- **Decidable:** with a tagger, plus the exception for words that are also in a
  technical noun category.
- **Code:** not implemented.

## Discrete form

```
check(text, glossary G) -> [finding]

for each word w of text that G lists as a technical verb:
    p = the part of speech that the sentence gives w
    if p is verb:                                              continue
    if p is noun and G also lists w as a technical noun:        continue   # rule 1.5
    if p is noun: finding(w)

pass = findings == []
```

## Examples

| Verdict | Text | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pass | `Ream the hole to a diameter of 10 mm.` | "ream" as a technical verb |
| pass | `Use a carbide drill.` | "drill" is also a technical noun (category 3) |
| fail | `Do the ream of the hole.` | "ream" as a noun |

## Out of scope

- A technical noun used as a verb — rule 1.7, the mirror of this rule.
- An `-ing` form used as a noun — rule 3.5, which permits it only as a technical
  noun or as a modifier in one.

## Notes

- The mirror pair 1.7/1.13 exists because a word that changes part of speech
  without changing form is the main source of ambiguity that STE removes. Both
  need the same input: a glossary that records categories per part of speech.
