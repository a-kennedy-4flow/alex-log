# Rule 9.3 — No phrasal verbs

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-9-7)
> When you use two words together, do not make phrasal verbs.

- **Applies to:** every verb of the text that is followed by a preposition or an
  adverb.
- **Needs:** dictionary (the phrasal verbs that are approved), parser, a list of
  known phrasal verbs.
- **Decidable:** yes against a list, and the list has to exist because the standard
  says you will not usually find phrasal verbs in the dictionary as "not approved".
- **Code:** not implemented.

## Discrete form

```
check(text, dictionary D, phrasal_verbs P) -> [finding]

for each verb v of text followed by a particle p:
    if D approves "v p" as a multi-word entry:  continue    # "put on", "come on"
    if P lists "v p":                           finding("v p", use: P.replacement)
    if p is a preposition that belongs to the
       object of v ("go through the door"):     continue
    else: report("v p", :possible_phrasal_verb, severity: :question)

pass = findings == []
```

## Examples

| Verdict | Text | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pass | `Put on protective clothing.` | "put on" is one of the few approved phrasal verbs, with a restricted meaning |
| pass | `A dim light comes on.` | "come on" is approved |
| fail | `After you put out the fire, close the valve on the fire extinguisher.` | "put out" → `extinguish` |
| fail | `This compound can give off poisonous fumes.` | "give off" → `release` |

Both rejected examples are made of approved words: "put", "out", "give", and "off"
are all in the dictionary. The phrase is what is not approved, because its meaning
is not the meaning of its parts.

## Notes

- Phrasal verbs are also the mechanism behind several rule 1.3 findings ("go down",
  "go through"), so one list can serve both rules. Keep the entries tagged with which
  rule reports them: a phrase that has a different meaning from its parts is 9.3, a
  single word used outside its approved meaning is 1.3.
- Because the check is a list lookup, its coverage is the size of the list. The
  honest output for an unknown verb-plus-particle pair is a question, not a pass.
