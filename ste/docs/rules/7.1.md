# Rule 7.1 — Identify the level of risk

**Statement** (Issue 9, page 1-7-2)
> Use an applicable word (for example, "warning" or "caution") to identify the level
> of risk.

- **Applies to:** every safety instruction.
- **Needs:** structure (which blocks are safety instructions), judgment (is the
  level right?).
- **Decidable:** the presence of the word yes; the correctness of the level no,
  because it comes from a risk analysis.
- **Code:** not implemented.

## Discrete form

```
check(text, structure S, signal_words W) -> [finding]

for each safety instruction b of S:
    if b starts with a word of W or carries a symbol of W:  continue
    else: finding(b, :no_signal_word, use: W)

for each safety instruction b with signal word w:
    if b names injury, death, or a risk to persons and w is a caution:
        finding(b, :level_too_low, use: "warning")

pass = findings == []
```

## The definitions

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| warning | there is a risk of injury or death |
| caution | there is a risk of damage to objects |

The decision rule of the standard:

- risk of injury or death → warning
- risk of damage to machines, tools, or equipment → caution
- both levels together → warning

`W` is configurable because other industries use other words ("danger",
"attention", "notice") or graphical symbols; the standard says that whatever words
you use must obey rules 7.1 through 7.3.

## The example

| Verdict | Text |
| --- | --- |
| fail | `CAUTION: EXTREME CLEANLINESS OF OXYGEN TUBES IS IMPERATIVE.` |
| pass | `WARNING: MAKE SURE THAT THE OXYGEN TUBES ARE FULLY CLEAN. OXYGEN AND GREASE MAKE AN EXPLOSIVE MIXTURE. AN EXPLOSION CAN CAUSE INJURY OR DEATH.` |

The caution is wrong twice: the level is too low for a risk of explosion, and the
text is abstract (rule 4.1) instead of telling the reader what to do (rule 7.2) and
what can happen (rule 7.3).

## Notes

- The `:level_too_low` half is a keyword test on the words that name a consequence
  ("injury", "death", "explosion", "poisonous", "burn", "suffocation", "electric
  shock"). It will miss risks that the text does not name, which is exactly why the
  standard asks for a risk analysis first.
- STE gives no rules for formatting, so uppercase text in the examples is not a
  requirement. Do not build the check on the case of the letters.
